## 1. Your right to a Good Faith Estimate
You have the right to receive a "Good Faith Estimate" explaining how much your health care will cost.

Under the law, health care providers need to give patients who don't have certain types of health care coverage, or who are not using certain types of health care coverage, an estimate of their expected bill for health care items and services before those items or services are provided.

- You have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate for the total expected cost of any health care items or services upon request or when scheduling such items or services. This includes related costs like medical tests, prescription drugs, equipment and hospital fees.
- If you receive a bill that is at least $400 more for any provider or facility than your Good Faith Estimate from that provider or facility, you can dispute the bill.
- Make sure to save a copy or picture of your Good Faith Estimate and your bill.

## 2. Who this notice applies to
This notice applies if you do not have health insurance, or if you have insurance but choose not to use it for a visit (a self-pay visit). If you have a plan we are in network with and want a self-pay visit, you must tell us before the visit and agree in writing that we will not submit the visit to your plan. This option is not available to anyone with Medicare, including a Medicare Advantage plan we are in network with.

Medicare. If you have Original Medicare (Part A or Part B), federal rules require that Medicare-covered visits be billed to Medicare, and we do not currently bill Original Medicare. If you have a Medicare Advantage plan we are not in network with, federal rules limit what we may charge for your visit. In either case, please do not book a self-pay visit. If you have a Medicare Advantage plan we are in network with, book using your plan. Not sure which you have? Check your card or call the number on it.

## 3. Your estimate at TeleDirectMD

### 3.1 The expected charge
A self-pay video visit with Parth Bhavsar, MD costs a flat $79, paid when you book. It covers the video visit, the evaluation and treatment plan, sending any prescription electronically to your pharmacy, and an excuse note if one is medically appropriate. It does not include the cost of any medicine, which your pharmacy charges separately (Section 3.4).

### 3.2 When you get your written estimate
- We send a written Good Faith Estimate with every self-pay booking confirmation.
- If you ask for an estimate before you book, we send it in writing within 3 business days after you ask.
- You can ask for a copy at any time by emailing contact@teledirectmd.com or calling (678) 956-1855.

### 3.3 What each estimate lists
Each estimate includes the items federal rules require, including your name and date of birth, a description of the service, the expected charge, and the name, National Provider Identifier (NPI) and tax identification number of the practice that provides care in your state. The practice depends on where you are during the visit: TeleDirectMD (a California professional medical corporation) for California, Parth Bhavsar Medicine PLLC (doing business as TeleDirectMD) for New York, TeleDirectMD, PLLC for Texas, and Mahavir Health Services LLC (doing business as TeleDirectMD) for all other states we serve and Washington, D.C.

### 3.4 Costs from other providers
Our estimate does not include services from other providers, such as lab tests, imaging, medicines from your pharmacy, or specialist or in-person care after a referral. Those providers bill you separately, and you have the right to ask each of them for their own Good Faith Estimate.

A Good Faith Estimate is not a contract, and it does not require you to receive the service.

## 4. Timing rules under federal law
If you schedule a health care item or service at least 3 business days in advance, your health care provider must give you a Good Faith Estimate in writing within 1 business day after scheduling. If you schedule at least 10 business days in advance, your provider must give you the estimate within 3 business days after scheduling. You can also ask any health care provider for a Good Faith Estimate before you schedule an item or service, and the provider must give you the estimate within 3 business days after you ask.

## 5. If your bill is higher than your estimate
- If a bill from a provider or facility is at least $400 more than that provider's or facility's Good Faith Estimate, you can use the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process.
- You must start the dispute within 120 calendar days (about 4 months) of the date on the original bill.
- The dispute process has an administrative fee. The amount is set by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).
- An independent reviewer looks at your Good Faith Estimate and your bill and decides what you should pay.
- While your dispute is open, we will not send your bill to collections or threaten to. If a bill is already in collections, we will stop collection efforts until the dispute is decided, and we will not add late fees while the dispute is open.
- Starting a dispute will not affect the care we provide you, and we will not take any action against you for disputing a bill.

## 7. Resumen en español
