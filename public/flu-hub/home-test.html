Testing at home

Home flu or COVID test: what your result means and what to do next

A home test can point you to the right treatment, but a negative result does not rule out flu. Here is what to do with each result.

Clinical editor Parth Bhavsar, MDFollows FDA home test authorizations; CDC testing guidanceSources checked October 5, 2026

If your test is positive for flu

  • Higher risk, pregnant, or getting worse: contact a clinician today. CDC recommends antiviral treatment as soon as possible for these groups.[5] Who is higher risk.[7]
  • Healthy adult within 2 days of symptoms: an antiviral is an option you can ask about.[5]
  • Healthy and past 2 days, improving: rest at home and watch for warning signs.
  • A child: call the child's pediatrician.

A home test does not come with a prescription. You still need a clinician, and you do not need a second test first: CDC says testing is not required to prescribe an antiviral.[4]

If your test is positive for COVID-19

COVID-19 has its own antiviral treatments, which need to start within 5 to 7 days of symptoms.[6] Contact a clinician, especially if you are older or have a higher-risk condition.

If your test is negative but you have symptoms

Do not take a negative result as the final word. Rapid antigen flu tests miss many cases: CDC lists sensitivity of about 53% to 54% for antigen tests read by eye.[3] FDA advises that anyone who tests negative and still has fever, cough, or shortness of breath may have flu, COVID-19, or another infection and should follow up.[1]

  • Higher risk: call a clinician anyway. Treatment decisions should not wait for a positive test.[5]
  • Checking for COVID-19: test again 48 hours later, for at least two tests.[2]
  • Everyone: stay home until you meet CDC's return rule, whatever the result.[8] How long flu is contagious.

A home test result also helps you protect others: if you live with someone at high risk, a positive flu result is a reason to ask about preventive antivirals for them.

Common questions

I tested positive for flu at home. Do I need another test?

Usually not. CDC says testing is not required to prescribe an antiviral, and treatment should not be delayed for results.[4] Tell the clinician which test you used and when.

My home test is negative but I feel awful. Could it still be flu?

Yes. Rapid antigen tests miss many flu cases, so a negative result does not rule flu out.[3] FDA advises that people who test negative and still have symptoms may have flu, COVID-19, or another infection and should follow up with a clinician.[1]

Should I test again?

For COVID-19, FDA recommends testing again 48 hours after a negative antigen test if you have symptoms, for at least two tests.[2] For flu, if you are in a higher-risk group, call a clinician rather than waiting on a second test.[5]
