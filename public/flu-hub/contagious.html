Contagious period

How long is flu contagious, and when can I go back to work or school?

Flu spreads from about a day before symptoms until roughly 5 to 7 days after, and most easily in the first 3 days. CDC's return rule is based on how you feel, not a fixed number of days.

CDC's return rule

You can go back to normal activities, including work and school, when for at least 24 hours both are true:[2]

  • your symptoms are getting better overall, and
  • you have not had a fever, without using fever-reducing medicine.

For the next 5 days, take added precautions when you are around other people: cleaner air, good hand and cough hygiene, a well-fitted mask, distance, or testing.[2]

Fever-reducing medicine such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen can hide a fever, so the 24 hours count only when you have not taken it. More on symptom relief.

School and childcare

CDC uses the same rule for students and school staff: no fever for 24 hours without medicine, and symptoms improving for 24 hours.[3] State and local health departments may have extra rules, and schools are told to follow them too.[3] Daycare and childcare programs may have their own policies, so check before sending a child back.

Healthcare workers

The rule is stricter for people who work in healthcare. CDC's infection control guidance keeps them off work until at least 3 days have passed since symptoms started, they have been fever-free for 24 hours without medicine, symptoms are improving, and they feel well enough. They then wear a mask at work through the end of day 7.[4] Your employer's occupational health team has the final word.

Do antivirals make you less contagious?

What is known:

  • In a large trial, a single dose of baloxavir lowered the amount of virus a day after starting more than oseltamivir or placebo did.[7]
  • In a 2025 household trial, people treated with baloxavir within 48 hours passed lab-confirmed flu to household members less often: 9.5% versus 13.4% with placebo by day 5. The difference in household members who actually got sick was smaller and could have been chance.[8]
  • The same trial notes that studies of oseltamivir and related drugs have not shown enough evidence that they prevent spread to contacts.[8]

What is not known: whether taking any antiviral means you can safely return sooner. CDC's return rule does not change if you take one.[2] You may see a claim that Tamiflu makes you contagious for one day less. FluHub could not trace that number to a primary study, so we do not repeat it as fact.

The rule in one line

24 hours with no fever (without medicine) and feeling better overall, then 5 days of added precautions.[2]

Common questions

Am I still contagious after my fever breaks?

You can be. CDC notes that you may still be able to spread the virus even when you feel better, which is why it advises 5 days of added precautions after you return to normal activities.[2]

Can I go to work or school with flu if I have no fever?

Not everyone with flu has a fever.[6] CDC's rule needs both: no fever for 24 hours without fever-reducing medicine, and symptoms improving overall for 24 hours.[2] If you have no fever but are still getting worse, stay home.

Does Tamiflu make me less contagious?

It has not been shown to. A 2025 trial noted that studies of oseltamivir-type drugs have not shown enough evidence that they prevent spread to others. The same trial found a single dose of baloxavir modestly lowered lab-confirmed spread in households.[8] Neither medicine changes CDC's return rule.[2]

How long after exposure do flu symptoms start?

Usually 1 to 4 days.[5] You can spread flu about a day before your own symptoms begin.[1]

Is my child contagious longer?

Possibly. CDC says young children and people with weakened immune systems may be contagious for longer.[1] Schools and childcare programs may also follow state or local rules in addition to CDC's.[3]
