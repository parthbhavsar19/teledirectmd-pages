Medicine safety

Tamiflu (oseltamivir) side effects

Most people have no side effects or mild stomach upset. The behavior warning on the label is real, but the best recent evidence suggests flu itself, not the medicine, drives most of that risk.

Clinical editor Parth Bhavsar, MDFollows Tamiflu FDA label; JAMA Neurology 2025Sources checked October 5, 2026

Common side effects

Side effectHow often (treatment trials)
Nausea, adultsAbout 10%[1]
Vomiting, adultsAbout 8%[1]
Headache, adultsAbout 2%[1]
Vomiting, children16%, versus 8% on placebo[1]

Taking each dose with food may make it easier to tolerate.[1] CDC also lists nausea and vomiting as the most common side effects.[4]

Rare but serious

  • Allergic and skin reactions. Anaphylaxis and serious skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, have been reported. Stop the medicine and get care right away for swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing, or a blistering or peeling rash.[1]
  • Confusion or unusual behavior. See below.

The behavior warning, in context

The FDA label says there have been reports after approval of delirium and abnormal behavior leading to injury, in some cases fatal, in people with flu who were taking Tamiflu. It also says "the contribution of TAMIFLU to these events has not been established," and that flu itself "can be associated with a variety of neurologic and behavioral symptoms," including hallucinations, delirium, and abnormal behavior.[1] Most reports came from children in Japan.[3]

A large 2025 study helps separate the two. Vanderbilt researchers followed 692,295 children and teens aged 5 to 17 in Tennessee Medicaid over several flu seasons and counted serious neurologic and psychiatric events that led to a hospital stay.[2]

  • Compared with flu that was not treated, these events were about half as common while flu was being treated with oseltamivir (rate ratio 0.53).[2]
  • The lower rate was driven mostly by fewer neurologic events rather than psychiatric ones.[2]
  • The authors concluded the findings support oseltamivir for preventing these flu-related complications.[2]

This was an observational study, so it cannot prove cause and effect, and the label warning still stands. The practical advice is the same either way: watch anyone with flu, especially a child or teen, for sudden confusion or unusual behavior, and get care right away if it happens.[1]

Other things to know

  • The nasal spray flu vaccine should not be given within 2 weeks before or 48 hours after Tamiflu unless a clinician decides it is needed. The shot is not affected.[1]
  • Tamiflu does not replace the yearly flu vaccine.[1]
  • People with kidney disease usually need a lower dose.[1]

Get care right away for

sudden confusion, hallucinations, or unusual behavior; a seizure; swelling of the face or throat; or a blistering rash.[1]

For a child

Call the child's pediatrician with any questions about side effects or dosing.

Common questions

What are the most common side effects of Tamiflu?

Nausea and vomiting. In adult treatment trials, nausea affected about 10% and vomiting about 8% of people; in children, vomiting affected 16% compared with 8% on placebo.[1]

Should I take Tamiflu with food?

It can be taken with or without food, but the label says it may be better tolerated with food.[1]

Can Tamiflu cause hallucinations or strange behavior in kids?

The label describes rare reports of delirium and abnormal behavior in people with flu taking Tamiflu, and says the drug's role has not been established. Flu itself can cause these symptoms.[1] A 2025 study of 692,295 children found serious neuropsychiatric events were about half as common during treated flu as during untreated flu.[2]

When should I stop Tamiflu and get help?

Get care right away for signs of a serious allergic or skin reaction, such as swelling, trouble breathing, or a blistering or peeling rash, or for confusion or unusual behavior.[1]
