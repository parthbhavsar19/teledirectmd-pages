Prevention

Find a flu shot

Flu shots are available at pharmacies, doctors' offices, clinics, health departments, and community health centers. Most insurance covers them with no copay.

Notice, updated October 5, 2026: the federal vaccines.gov ZIP search is not working

The search tool on vaccines.gov has returned an error for ZIP code searches since at least December 2025, and HHS has not said when it will be fixed (reported October 1, 2026).[8] FluHub's directory below does not use that live tool; it is built from an archived copy of the same provider list. For current hours and stock, use the other ways to find a shot below.

Your doctor or clinic

The best option if you also want other vaccines or have health conditions to discuss. Call or use their patient portal.

Pharmacies

Most chain and independent pharmacies take walk-ins or same-day online bookings through their own websites and apps. The youngest age a pharmacist can vaccinate varies by state.[11]

    Other ways to find a shot

    Bringing a child? Call first: state law sets the youngest age a pharmacist can vaccinate. As of January 2025, only 19 states plus Washington, DC allowed pharmacists to give all recommended vaccines to children 3 and older.[11]

    Your own clinician

    Your doctor's office or patient portal can often book a shot with your next visit, and it is the best place to ask which vaccine fits you.

    Local health department

    Many run free or low-cost fall clinics, and they give Vaccines for Children vaccine to eligible kids.[5] Search the name of your county or city with "health department flu clinic."

    Community health centers

    Low or no cost, with or without insurance. Search HRSA's directory.[3]

    FluMist Home

    For 2026-27 the nasal spray ships to the 48 contiguous states for eligible people ages 2 to 49: adults 18 to 49 can give it to themselves, and a parent or caregiver gives it to children 2 to 17.[9] In some states your own doctor must approve the order first, and there is a shipping fee.[10] flumist.com, or who should not use it.

    Before you go

    • Bring your insurance card. Marketplace plans, most private plans, and Medicare Part B cover the flu vaccine with no copay at an in-network or participating provider.[6][7]
    • If you are 65 or older, ask for a high-dose, adjuvanted, or recombinant vaccine, but take whatever is available rather than leave without one.[5]
    • Ideally get vaccinated by the end of October. Later in the season is still worthwhile.[2]

    Questions about safety, side effects, or egg allergy: flu shot facts. For children: is my child's flu shot still recommended and free?
