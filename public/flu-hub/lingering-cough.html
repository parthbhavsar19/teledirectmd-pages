Recovery

Lingering cough and tiredness after flu

The fever is gone but the cough hangs on, and you still feel worn out. That is common. Here is what a normal recovery looks like and which symptoms mean you should be checked.

Clinical editor Parth Bhavsar, MDFollows CDC flu symptoms; CMAJ 2024Sources checked October 5, 2026

A normal recovery

Time since symptoms beganWhat is typical
First few days to 2 weeksMost people recover from flu in this window.[1]
3 to 8 weeksA leftover cough is common: 11% to 25% of adults have one after a respiratory infection. The infection leaves the airways more sensitive and making more mucus for a while.[2]
More than 8 weeksNo longer considered a post-infection cough. It needs a check for other causes such as asthma or COPD.[2]

Easing the cough

Reviews of clinical trials found no proof that inhaled steroids, inhalers that open the airways, or oral medicines speed up recovery from this kind of cough. Knowing it is expected and will pass is the main treatment.[2] For short-term relief of other symptoms, see symptom relief.

Tiredness

Fatigue is one of flu's main symptoms.[1] Feeling worn out for a while after the fever ends is common, and most people recover within a few days to less than two weeks.[1]

Rarely, flu can inflame the heart muscle or lead to other serious complications.[1] Tiredness together with chest pain, shortness of breath, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or fainting is not normal recovery and should be checked promptly.

Red flags: get checked

  • A fever or cough that improved and then came back or got worse. This is a CDC emergency warning sign.[1] Getting worse after getting better.
  • Coughing up blood, trouble swallowing, marked shortness of breath, a hoarse voice, or whole-body symptoms such as fever or weight loss.[2]
  • Coughing fits, vomiting after coughing, or a "whoop" when breathing in, which can be whooping cough.[2]
  • A cough lasting more than 8 weeks.[2]
  • A long-term condition such as asthma, COPD, heart failure, or diabetes that is getting worse.[3]

Go to an emergency department or call 911 for

trouble breathing, chest pain or pressure, confusion, a seizure, not urinating, or a fever or cough that got better and then came back worse. All warning signs

Back to work?

CDC's return rule is based on fever and whether your symptoms are improving overall.[4] How long flu is contagious

Common questions

How long does a cough last after flu?

Most people recover from flu within a few days to less than two weeks.[1] A cough that lingers after a respiratory infection is common: it affects about 11% to 25% of adults and can last 3 to 8 weeks.[2]

Will an inhaler or cough medicine make it go away faster?

Reviews of trials of inhaled steroids, bronchodilators, and oral medicines for this kind of cough found no evidence of benefit. Most cases settle without treatment.[2]

When is a lingering cough a problem?

See a clinician if you cough up blood, have trouble swallowing, are very short of breath, have a hoarse voice, or have whole-body symptoms such as fever or weight loss. A cough lasting more than 8 weeks needs a check-up.[2]
